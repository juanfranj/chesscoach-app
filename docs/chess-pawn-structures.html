Chess pawn structures: the 25 that decide the middlegame

When the opening theory runs out, the pawns tell you what to do. Each structure comes with its own plans, breaks and good squares, and recognising it is half of finding the right move. Here are the 25 that Chess Coach teaches, and how to practise them.

Why pawn structures matter

Pieces move around; pawns stay. The pawn structure decides which files open, which squares your pieces can use for good and where each side should attack. Two positions from different openings with the same structure usually want the same plan, which is why strong players think in structures rather than in move orders.

The 25 structures

StructureHow to recognise it
The Benko structureBlack plays with no pawns on the a- and b-files.
The asymmetric BenoniA white pawn on d5 locked against the black pawn on d6, with c5 beside it and a black bishop fianchettoed on g7.
The symmetrical BenoniWhite pawns on c4 and d5 against Black's on c5 and d6, with the d-file locked.
The Carlsbad structureWhite with pawns on d4 and e3 and a kingside majority; Black with c6, d5 and a queenside majority.
The Caro-Kann formationWhite pawns on c3 and d4, Black pawns on c6 and e6.
The French chainTwo pawn chains locked head to head.
The traded centre in the FrenchWhite pawns on c3 and d4, Black pawns on c6 and d5, and the e-pawns already traded.
The French with f6An isolated white pawn on d4 against d5, while Black has no f- or c-pawn: a half-open f-file and a lone pawn on e6.
The Grünfeld centreWhite pawns on c3, d4 and e4, with no white pawn left on the b-file.
Hanging pawnsTwo pawns side by side on c5 and d5, no neighbours and nothing in front of them.
The HedgehogBlack pawns on a6, b6, d6 and e6, no c-pawn, and the castled pawns untouched on f7, g7 and h7.
The isolated queen pawnA pawn on d4 with no neighbours on the c- or e-files, facing a black pawn on d5 or an empty square.
The small c6 and d6 centreWhite has pawns on c4 and e4 and none on the d-file; Black has c6 and d6 and none on the e-file.
The open centre after the tradeA black pawn on e5 facing White's c4 and e4 pawns, with the d-file empty of pawns.
The d5 against e5 chainWhite pawns on c4, d5 and e4 against black pawns on c7, d6 and e5.
The e5 and f5 duoA locked centre, d5 against d6: Black has the d6–e5 chain and a pawn on f5.
The race on both wingsA locked centre, d5 and e4 against d6 and e5, with the c-pawns traded: Black pushes on the kingside, White on the queenside.
The majority that makes a passerOne side has more pawns on one wing than the other.
The Maróczy bindWhite pawns on c4 and e4 that take b5 and d5 away from Black.
The d5 wedgeA white pawn on d5 locked against Black's d6 pawn, with another black pawn on e5.
The Scheveningen small centreBlack keeps the pawns on d6 and e6 side by side and unadvanced.
The d5 square in the SicilianBlack has pawns on d6 and e5 and has already traded the c-pawn.
The Slav formationWhite has d4 and e3 and is left with no c-pawn.
The Closed SpanishWhite pawns on c3, d3 and e4; Black pawns on a6, b5, c5, d6 and e5.
The StonewallPawns on c6, d5, e6 and f5 forming a wall on the light squares.

Questions

What are the most important pawn structures in chess?
The isolated queen pawn, hanging pawns, the Carlsbad structure, the French chain, the Maróczy bind and the d5 square in the Sicilian come up in a huge number of openings. They are a good place to start.
What is the plan against an isolated queen pawn?
Blockade the square in front of it, trade pieces and aim at the pawn in the endgame. The side with the isolated pawn wants the opposite: keep pieces on and attack before the weakness counts.
Do I need to know openings to understand structures?
No. It works the other way round: knowing the structure an opening leads to tells you what to do once the theory ends.
