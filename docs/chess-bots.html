Bots that lose like people, not like broken engines

The usual way to make an engine weaker is to make it play a random bad move now and then, which feels nothing like a real opponent. Chess Coach does it differently: the mistakes a rival makes are chosen among the moves the engine itself was considering, so they are the plausible slips a player of that rating makes.

The four advanced rivals are Maia, a neural network trained on millions of real online games to predict the move a player of a given rating would make. They open, defend and blunder the way club players do. The master and grandmaster rivals are full engines, limited only as much as their rating needs.

After the game

Every game against a bot goes to your list, where the engine marks the moves that cost you something and the AI coach can explain them. That is what turns a lost game into the next thing to work on.

Questions

Is it free to play against the bots?
Yes. Playing against the bots and the engine review of your games are free. There are no ads.
Which bot should I play against?
One slightly above your rating. If you win most games, move up; if you lose most, move down one. If you are just starting, begin with Beto.
Can I play against bots offline?
Yes. The engines run on your phone, so you can play without a connection.
Are there bots that play like humans?
Yes. The four advanced rivals are Maia, a network trained to predict what real players of a given rating play, mistakes included.
