What the lessons are made of

The lessons mix several kinds, and the later courses add more:

  • A short piece of theory, with the idea on a board, not a wall of text.
  • Puzzles on that idea, so you use it straight away.
  • Openings to learn along the way, a little at a time.
  • Endgames, from the Intermediate course on, played against the engine.
  • Games against bots to put the idea to work. Win for all three stars.

In the Intermediate and Advanced courses, some sections open after you play an online game against another person.

Why a course and not just puzzles

Puzzles train what you already know. When you are starting out, the hard part is knowing what to learn and in what order: the checkmates before the openings, the basic endgames before the long plans. A course puts that order in front of you and lets you go at your own pace.

What is free

The whole First Steps course is free, and so are playing against the bots and the engine review of your games. In the other courses you can try the first lessons for free; the rest is Premium.

Questions

Can I learn chess on my own with an app?
Yes, as long as the app gives you an order to follow and games to test yourself. In Chess Coach the courses include games against bots to put each idea to work, and after every game the app shows you what went wrong.
How long does it take to learn chess?
The rules take an afternoon; the First Steps course covers them in 29 short lessons. Playing well takes longer, which is why the path goes on through three more courses, past a 2000 rating.
Is it good for children?
The First Steps course starts from zero and uses short lessons, so it works for children who can read. There are no ads.
Does it work on iPhone and Android?
Yes, on iPhone, iPad and Android, and your progress follows you across devices when you sign in.
