Essential chess endgames: the 100 to know, and a way to practise them

Knowing that the Lucena position wins and the Philidor position draws is one thing. Doing it against someone who defends perfectly is another. Here is the list, family by family, and the way Chess Coach lets you play every one of them against an engine that does not slip.

Where to start

Start with king and pawn endings. Every other endgame eventually turns into one, and the three ideas they teach decide most of them: the opposition, the rule of the square and the critical squares in front of a pawn.

Then go to rook endings. They are the ones you will meet most often in your own games, which is why they take 42 of the 100 places here: the Lucena and Philidor positions, cutting the king off, the short-side defence and the Vancura defence.

Bishop, queen and knight endings come last. Some you have to win; many are about knowing which positions are a draw and holding them.

How to practise them in Chess Coach

  1. Pick an endgame. The families are ordered from easiest to hardest, and your progress is kept for each one.
  2. Read the goal and the technique. Every endgame tells you whether you have to win or hold, and explains the method in a few short steps.
  3. Play it against the engine at full strength. Every position has been checked against endgame tablebases, so the result on offer is the real one.
  4. Choose guided or free. In guided mode the app warns you the moment a move throws the result away; in free mode you find out at the end.
  5. Then practise. Once you know the technique, the practice mode serves a fresh position every time, drawn from more than 45,000 positions from real games.

Questions

Which chess endgames should I learn first?
The basic mates with queen and rook, then king and pawn endings: the opposition, the rule of the square and the critical squares. After that, the Lucena and Philidor positions, because rook endings are the ones you will meet most.
How many endgames do I really need to know?
Around a hundred positions cover the endgame theory a club player needs, and that is the size of the list here. What matters more than the number is being able to play them, not just recognise them.
Can I practise endgames against a computer for free?
Yes. In Chess Coach six king and pawn endgames are free to learn, and king and pawn practice positions are unlimited. The rest of the endgames are Premium.
Does it work on iPhone and Android?
Yes, on iPhone, iPad and Android. The engine runs on your device, so you can practise without a connection.
